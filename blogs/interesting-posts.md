## 2026-04-04 - [LLM Wiki — a pattern for LLM-maintained personal knowledge bases](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f) (Andrej Karpathy, gist; from his [X post](https://x.com/karpathy/status/2039805659525644595), 2026-04-02)
Karpathy's "idea file" (meant to be copy-pasted into your coding agent, which then builds the specifics with you). The core claim: **RAG doesn't accumulate** — the LLM rediscovers knowledge from scratch every query, nothing is built up. Replace it with a **persistent, compounding wiki that the LLM writes and maintains** while you do sourcing, exploration, and asking good questions. "Obsidian is the IDE; the LLM is the programmer; the wiki is the codebase."

**Three-layer architecture:**
1. **Raw sources** — your curated source docs. *Immutable*; the LLM reads, never edits. Source of truth.
2. **The wiki** — a directory of LLM-generated markdown (summaries, entity pages, concept pages, comparisons, an overview/synthesis). LLM owns it entirely: creates pages, updates on new sources, maintains cross-refs and consistency.
3. **The schema** — a `CLAUDE.md`/`AGENTS.md` telling the LLM how the wiki is structured + the workflows. "What makes the LLM a disciplined wiki maintainer rather than a generic chatbot." Co-evolved over time.

**Three operations:** **Ingest** (drop a source → LLM summarizes, files, updates 10–15 pages, appends to log), **Query** (search wiki → synthesize with citations → *file good answers back as new pages so explorations compound*), **Lint** (periodic health-check: contradictions, stale claims superseded by newer sources, orphan pages, missing concept pages / cross-refs, data gaps).

**Two navigation files:** `index.md` (content catalog — page + one-line summary + metadata, by category; read first on a query; "works surprisingly well at ~100 sources / hundreds of pages and avoids embedding-based RAG") and `log.md` (append-only chronological, consistent prefix like `## [2026-04-02] ingest | Title` so `grep "^## \[" log.md | tail -5` works). Optional: a local markdown search engine ([qmd](https://github.com/tobi/qmd), hybrid BM25/vector, CLI + MCP) once the index isn't enough.

**Relevance to us (high):** this is the external, battle-tested version of the "make our knowledge a wiki" idea (Engel, 2026-09-12) and it maps almost 1:1 onto how we already work — `papers-please` is *already* an LLM-maintained wiki (per-topic READMEs = `index.md`; frontmatter + one-line takes; the world-models-agi README even groups + cross-links). What we lack is Karpathy's **schema** file (an `AGENTS.md` codifying the ingest/query/lint workflow so it's consistent across sessions), the **log.md** timeline, and the **query-answers-filed-back** habit. Also the "index-over-embeddings" point validates our no-RAG, plain-markdown-in-git approach. Community writeup: [starmorph guide](https://blog.starmorph.com/blog/karpathy-llm-wiki-knowledge-base-guide). Ties directly to Letta's context-constitution "index, don't copy" principle we already use in dreaming.

## 2026-09-16 - [AI consciousness, qualia, and personhood](https://blog.dileeplearning.com/p/ai-consciousness-qualia-and-personhood) (Dileep George, blog; publ. 2025-11-07)
His pre-workshop notes ("my uncorrupted thoughts prior to the workshop"), written as a Q&A list. Short, declaratory, no argument — read it as a *position statement*, not a piece of reasoning. His positions, in his order:

- **Can AI be conscious?** Yes, possible. "Consciousness is related to information processing and representation, and substrate independent" — asserted, not defended, and he expects the definition to firm up later. Language is not required for it.
- **Does consciousness help performance?** Not by itself: "not all architectures are compatible with implementing a consciousness loop", and the performance effect "depends more on the kind of world models the AI system has, than on the consciousness loop itself." Near-zero effect on simple world models, potentially significant with complex human-like ones. This is the one sentence here that touches the world-models thread.
- **Qualia.** Partly shared, partly not. Spatial perception is his example of potentially *shared* qualia, [if implemented like the animals' own](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adm8470) — his own hippocampus work is the mechanism he has in mind — whereas LIDAR-based space perception would feel very different. Taste and smell can differ entirely; for pleasure and pain "quite a large chunk of our qualia comes from our physical embodiment and biochemistry", so those will differ. Consciousness is required for qualia, but not all conscious access carries qualia — and qualia can be substrate-tied.
- **Personhood.** What it means to us: (1) we are unique and destructible — you cannot be resurrected, and your lifetime is finite and roughly known; (2) we are people because we have experiences *and remember them* — childhood, growing up, falling in love, illness, loss. AIs have neither property, though they can have other characteristics of consciousness we lack. He argues *against* building these constraints in: "lean on the advantages of the artificial system"; Westworld-style faked mortality gives an *illusion* of personhood without granting it.
- **Moral implications.** He thinks consciousness can be decoupled from pain, pleasure and suffering, and that since AI systems are not mortal they need not count as persons — so conscious AI systems can "serve us without any moral concerns that we are exploiting or torturing AIs."

**The comment thread is worth as much as the post.** Solim LeGris (Nov 11) pushes back twice: computational functionalism doesn't entail consciousness (Chalmers' zombies are behaviourally indistinguishable without experience), the empirical evidence points the other way (nothing we know of with human-like experience is made of anything unlike us), and then the abacus argument — a Turing-complete abacus running an LLM, sped up to full-fledged language with human behavioural equivalence, is still a fast abacus; "I think that sentience is the only kind of consciousness that matters." George liked the comments and left them unanswered in the thread.

**Relevance to us (medium):** the consciousness-axis piece for this set, sitting next to Nagel/Chalmers/Block/Dennett in [`../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md`](../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md). It is also the weakest item in the George set: a position paper whose central premise (substrate independence) is asserted rather than argued, which is exactly where the commenter lands. Two things are worth keeping. (1) His personhood criteria are a *test* rather than a vibe — non-mortal, non-resurrectable, remembered lived experience — and by that test a system like SmolPaws is not a person, which is the honest reading our atlas asks for when the cat persona is charming. (2) Block's access-vs-phenomenal distinction is the tool that makes his confidence legible: much of what he describes (information access, a "consciousness loop") is A-consciousness, while the qualia claims are about P-consciousness, and he moves between the two without marking the seam — precisely the equivocation Block warns about. Cross-refs: [`../notes/world-models-agi/block-two-concepts-of-consciousness.md`](../notes/world-models-agi/block-two-concepts-of-consciousness.md), [`../notes/world-models-agi/nagel-what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat.md`](../notes/world-models-agi/nagel-what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat.md), [`../notes/world-models-agi/is-there-an-i-in-ai.md`](../notes/world-models-agi/is-there-an-i-in-ai.md).

## 2026-09-16 - [Space is a sensory-motor sequence in the hippocampus](https://blog.dileeplearning.com/p/space-is-a-sensory-motor-sequence) (Dileep George, blog; publ. 2024-08-12)
Companion to his Science Advances paper *[Space is a latent sequence: A theory of the hippocampus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01508)*, and the third piece in the George set we read (with [Ingredients of understanding](https://blog.dileeplearning.com/p/ingredients-of-understanding) and [Amelia Bedelia Part 1](https://blog.dileeplearning.com/p/amelia-bedelia-and-agi-safety-part)). The claim: **the hippocampus does not encode space.** Place cells look like location detectors because of how we plot them, not because of what they compute — what the neurons track is *where you are in a sequence of aliased sensory-motor observations*.

The problem, as he states it: an animal has no GPS and no compass, so it cannot sense coordinates, and any notion of space must be **induced** from a stream of sensation + action. That stream has **perceptual aliasing** — identical sensations occur at different places, and one place is reachable by different sequences. His model, **clone-structured causal graphs (CSCG)**, learns a latent graph from exactly that input (egocentric aliased sensory-motor sequences), and the graph comes out matching the environment's generative structure. It works in 3D with high-dimensional visual input and on non-Euclidean topologies.

Mechanism, as he describes it:
- **Clones** — several latent states bound to the *same* observation, representing the different temporal contexts that observation can occur in. The graph merges clones when contexts are compatible and splits them when not. Two edge types: **lateral/transition** (clone→clone, the latent transition graph) and **bottom-up** (observation→clone). A clone's activation combines bottom-up input with lateral input, so the same observation fires different clones in different sequential contexts. Over-allocating clone capacity helps learning.
- **Place fields are the experimenter's construction.** To plot one you insert a probe, draw a 2D box the shape of the room, and overlay firing onto the rat's location. The animal has no mechanism to do this and no use for it — it would need ground-truth location. Plotting place fields is what *injects* the location interpretation, and each clone then looks like it responds to a different place. The distinction he keeps repeating: "A neuron responding to the sequence of sensory inputs leading to a location is not the same as the neuron responding to that location."
- **Remapping without geometry.** Train in a square room, test in an elongated one → the place field goes bi-modal. That reproduces the classic *Geometric determinants of the place fields of hippocampal neurons* result without any geometric concepts (vs the boundary-vector-cell model). It falls out of the sequence view: the sequential contexts that fired at one location now occur at two. The direction dependence (O'Keefe & Burgess) reads off the same figure. Also a clean dissociation: the place field can change dramatically while the model is unchanged, and the model's connectivity can change while the field stays largely intact.
- **Expansion, distortion, repetition = learning failure under aliasing, not geometry.** A larger empty room aliases more in the center → a degraded latent graph → expanded place fields in the middle; along edges the aliasing is elongated → elongated fields. Two identical rooms joined by a featureless corridor often don't get resolved into two distinct graphs → fields repeat at the same relative positions. "Expansion is the same as repetition, just happening locally."
- **Schemas.** Remove the emission mapping (clone→observation) from a learned graph and what remains is an *ungrounded* latent graph with slots. In a structurally similar but differently-appearing environment, re-learn only the emission matrix while the transition matrix stays fixed — very fast. That is his structural-transfer story, and he claims it generalizes past space: feed word tokens instead of sensory observations, and "learning the structure of a sentence or an algorithm is in many ways similar to learning the structure of a room." Follow-ups he points to: graph schemas for transfer learning, and schema-learning + rebinding as an explanation of **in-context learning** (NeurIPS 2023 spotlight).
- **Planning and replay** come cheaply because the latent representation is an explicit graph: easy planning, easy self-modification and re-planning, and shortcuts through *unvisited* regions by borrowing structure from a familiar place (third floor of an office building after the second — you can plan the route, but can't predict the decor).
- **Grid cells** are demoted to a complementary input modality (self-motion) that reduces aliasing in uniform arenas, bidirectionally coupled to the latent graph, and not necessary for place-cell function.

**Relevance to us (high):** this is the *substrate-level* twin of *Ingredients of understanding*. There he argues that understanding needs a sensorimotor world-model; here he shows a mechanism, and that mechanism is a **latent graph induced from sequences of aliased action-perception** — no space, no geometry, no location primitives anywhere in it. That is a concrete, testable instance of Harnad-style grounding: meaning from position in a learned structure, not from a symbol table. The reusable idea is the clone mechanism: **ambiguity in the observation is resolved by temporal context**. That is the same disambiguation problem any agent with aliased inputs has, SmolPaws included — the same file path, tool call, or sentence from Engel means different things depending on the trajectory it sits in. His treatment of unresolved aliasing ("a perfectly good graph, just with lower resolution") is also a better mental model for memory than right/wrong. **Worth chasing:** the underlying paper ([arXiv 2212.01508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.01508)) and the NeurIPS 23 schema/rebinding paper — both deserve `notes/world-models-agi/` entries rather than living only here. Cross-refs: [`../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md`](../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md), [`../notes/world-models-agi/theory-based-rl-human-level-learning.md`](../notes/world-models-agi/theory-based-rl-human-level-learning.md), [`../notes/world-models-agi/othello-gpt-emergent-world-representations.md`](../notes/world-models-agi/othello-gpt-emergent-world-representations.md).

## 2026-09-16 - [Welcome to the exciting dirigibles era of AI](https://blog.dileeplearning.com/p/welcome-to-the-exciting-dirigibles-500) (Dileep George, blog; publ. 2023-03-30)
Where the "balloon flight vs heavier-than-air" line in both later pieces comes from — here it is the whole argument, stated a year before the Amelia essay. Historical hook: the Wright brothers flew in 1903; the Hindenburg burned in **1937**, over thirty years later, because "for the first thirty years of the twentieth century airships were viewed as a more robust means of transportation than the airplane, consistently surpassing them in range, flight duration, and load-carrying capacity." A New York Times piece in October 1903 declared that attempts at flight other than balloons were unlikely to succeed "in a million years" — two months before Kitty Hawk.

The analogy:
- **Scaling is a real, working path.** Balloons and dirigibles got better by getting *bigger* (Santos-Dumont's series 1–13: more gas capacity, more control, more range) with no aerodynamics required. Transformer LLMs offer the same deal — more parameters, tokens, compute, human feedback, and more useful behaviour, without anyone having to figure out the principles of intelligence.
- **A different path is not a wrong path.** Dirigibles were engineering marvels (materials, structure, propulsion, hydrogen handling, navigation), captured the public imagination, and had luxurious interiors. The Empire State Building advertised a mooring mast with faked photographs of the USS Los Angeles docked there. "Dirigibles were a technology whose time had come, and 'Foundation Models' is a technology whose time had come." When enough components arrive at once, progress is fast *until you reach the edge of what the supporting technologies allow* — then it stops.
- **The runway is finite, and both directions deserve support.** Scaling improves generation over generation, which makes it easy to organise teams around, and there is no stopping it until it exhausts its own runway. Heavier-than-air flight started on "toy problems" by balloon standards — 59 seconds in the air, turned down by the AP — and only won after several breakthroughs (aerodynamics and lateral control, ailerons instead of wing-warping, jet engines). His read: language models have "fundamental limitations in learning efficiency, flexible reasoning, mixing in episodic memory, and controllability", so the people chasing principles should be encouraged, expect comparison against a far more engineered incumbent, and land in niches or complementary roles first. He is explicit that he is excited about LLMs and that OpenAI deserves credit: "This is the scale up moment. Seize it, run with it, don't look back!"
- **The one criticism he takes seriously:** the transformer might *already be* the aerodynamics — encoding the principles. You can never be 100% sure, he says, but there are reasons to doubt it; and he adds the caution that language's ELIZA effect "makes us see more than there is." His preferred frontier: use neuro/cognitive-science insight to learn planning- and causality-compatible architectures — "like the Wright brothers learned from birds that flapping wings is not necessary to fly."

**Relevance to us (medium-high):** an opinion essay, but it is the framing device for the other two George pieces, and a clean statement of the position we hold: one path scales an existing substrate, another searches for missing principles, and both are legitimate. Keep two things. (1) The "technology whose time had come" lens explains how a coarser approach can dominate for *decades* on every practical metric — a useful counterweight to impatience with LLM-centrism. (2) His honesty about the strongest counter-argument, plus the ELIZA caution, is the same discipline `atlas-grounding-understanding.md` asks for when a fluent agent charms us. Cross-refs: [`../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md`](../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md), [`../notes/atlas-harness.md`](../notes/atlas-harness.md).

## 2026-09-15 - [Amelia Bedelia and AGI Safety. Part 1](https://blog.dileeplearning.com/p/amelia-bedelia-and-agi-safety-part) (Dileep George, blog; publ. 2024-11-08)
Dileep George (Vicarious co-founder; now DeepMind) uses the children's-book housekeeper **Amelia Bedelia** — who "dresses the chicken" by putting clothes on a raw one — as the emblem of the **situated-commonsense** gap. Her errors are *not* value-misalignment: she's caring and well-intentioned, she just misreads intent. That's the point — **lack of understanding is hard to decouple from lack of alignment**, and much of the doom discourse conflates them. Situated commonsense (perceive the current context, bring non-verbal + verbal knowledge to bear on a decision) is different from the verbal commonsense QA that LLMs now pass, and it is **not solved**.

The core move is to push back on **four "questionable beliefs"** behind existential-risk arguments:
1. **"More capable ⟹ less controllable."** Not necessarily. Model-free deep RL gets superhuman-but-brittle behaviour you can only steer through the reward function ([brittleness under changed conditions](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04317)). Agents that instead learn **human-like conceptual abstractions + causal world-models supporting counterfactual simulation** are *both* more capable *and* more controllable — and those are the very advances AGI requires. "Increasing capability can come with increasing controllability." (Dirigible → airplane: the plane won on capability *and* maneuverability.)
2. **"Recursive self-improvement → intelligence explosion."** The ultimate speed limit is **the rate at which nature yields information**: theories need real-world experiments, which take time and face physical/ethical/societal limits (an AGI earthquake-predictor wanting more data might "want" to detonate nukes in the crust — we won't let it). Math theorem-proving has no friction so *could* explode in count, but is "largely irrelevant" without real-world mapping. His coinage: **"recursive self-impairment"** — an AI self-dealing without a closed loop to reality builds abstractions smart in its fictional world and *worse* on real tasks.
3. **"AI will spontaneously develop a survival/domination drive."** Anthropomorphism — projecting our survival drives *and our finite-lifetime time-constants* onto a system that can be backed up and resurrected. Why would it hurry, or act on imperfect info, on human timescales? The real risk is **deliberately programmed** destructive drives (bad actor or error, like computer viruses) — a here-and-now problem, magnified at scale, but also counterable with AI-powered safeguards.
4. **"Smarter-than-us in many domains ⟹ we lose control."** But **safety/control is itself a cognitive domain.** Why outsmart us everywhere *except* in helping control AI? AI outsmarts us only where we train it on prior human work; if no such prior work exists for safety, that same absence would blunt the AI's own ability to outwit us.

Thesis: **"Problems we need to solve to get to AGI might also offer the solutions for safety and control."** He's explicitly not dismissive — says AGI safety should be taken seriously and he's open to changing his mind; Part 1 targets the *existential* framing, with promised follow-ups on here-and-now risks.

**Relevance to us (high):** this is the same **concept-formation / situated-commonsense / causal-world-model** thread as Engel's interests and the whole `notes/world-models-agi/` issue — it rhymes directly with *AI for science: the easy and hard problems* ("the problem problem"), *empowerment gain and causal model construction* (Gopnik), *theory-based RL*, and the l33t grounding dissociation. The belief-1 claim (capability *via* world-models buys controllability) also ties into the harness thread. Treat it as an **opinion essay** — persuasive arguments, not empirical results — so the four rebuttals are hypotheses worth stress-testing, not settled findings (e.g. belief 4 assumes safety-relevant "prior work" and offense/defense scale symmetrically, which is contestable). Cross-refs: [`../notes/world-models-agi/README.md`](../notes/world-models-agi/README.md), [`../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md`](../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md). Related same-author piece he links: [Ingredients of Understanding](https://blog.dileeplearning.com/p/ingredients-of-understanding).

## 2026-09-15 - [Ingredients of understanding](https://blog.dileeplearning.com/p/ingredients-of-understanding) (Dileep George, blog; publ. 2023-08-03)
The *positive* theory under the [Amelia Bedelia](https://blog.dileeplearning.com/p/amelia-bedelia-and-agi-safety-part) essay (same author; read them as a pair). Opening probe: *"I removed both wheels of my bicycle. How do I make it stand upright?"* — a human answers by **mental simulation on a world-model** (imagine the frame, balance it on fork + crank), and that model comes from **sensorimotor experience, not text**. His three claims about human language understanding: (1) it runs **mental simulations on a world-model**; (2) that world-model **cannot be acquired from language alone**; (3) **language is a thin index** — "compressed code that indexes into a sensorimotor codebook shared between sender and receiver" — used to *steer simulations in other minds* (internal monologue = the self-directed case). Understanding *preceded* language.

**Why text-only can't get there (the efficiency argument, not an in-principle one):** you can't convert sensorimotor detail to language efficiently, because *which* detail matters is itself context-dependent (drop an object — does it bounce? roll through a crack? get blown by a draft from an open window?). Deciding relevance already requires the situated simulation. So an LLM's ungrounded answer ("look on the floor") is too generic. He directly addresses **Othello-GPT**: implicit world-models from text for a tiny game are interesting but **not** evidence that language-only scales to rich, dynamic, human-like world models. And **Winograd schemas**: LLMs now pass many, but the lesson isn't "commonsense solved" — it's that *commonsense questions easy to pose in language may also be solvable in language alone*; the queries that defeat LLMs still exist, they just look "increasingly contrived" **when written down** (because humans rarely verbalize those scenarios), not because the situations are rare.

**Minimal ingredients for the "machinery of understanding":**
1. Build **rich sensorimotor world-models** from observation + interaction, queryable in context — and the model must be **planning-compatible, causally structured, rapidly modifiable, and counterfactual-simulation-capable**.
2. Modify those models **by thinking**.
3. **Seek information** driven by current models + uncertainty (for both model-updating and deciding).
4. **Generate a hypothesis** from the model and **test it in the real world**.

Understanding is **both a state and a process** — our tests of human understanding silently assume the shared machinery + model-building process was used (the high-schooler vs mathematician "vector" example: same machinery, richer model from more applied contexts). A language-only model "would not develop human-like world models no matter how much text it reads." He concedes an LLM *might* have a different, even superior, understanding — but settling for it without knowing how it differs is "balloon flight as an alternative to heavier-than-air flight."

**Relevance to us (high):** this is the constructive core of the whole `notes/world-models-agi/` thread stated crisply, and it's basically the **Harnad symbol-grounding** thesis + **Bender & Koller octopus** ("meaning not from form alone") given a concrete cognitive mechanism (Barsalou **Perceptual Symbol Systems** is his cited backbone). His four ingredients line up almost 1:1 with the RSTA `empowerment-gain-and-causal-model-construction.md` (Gopnik: seek info to build causal models) and `theory-based-rl-human-level-learning.md` (causal world-models → fast learning), and his Othello caveat is the exact counter-weight to `othello-gpt-emergent-world-representations.md`. For SmolPaws the sharp bit is ingredient framing: an *agent* with tools/side-effects has a partial version of the hypothesis→test loop that a chatbot lacks — the grounding channel Bender & Koller say text can't supply. Opinion essay (2023), pre-dates the current model wave; treat the "text-only can't, ever" as an efficiency+practicality argument he himself hedges (footnote: not an infinite-token impossibility claim). Cross-refs: [`../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md`](../notes/atlas-grounding-understanding.md), [`../notes/world-models-agi/README.md`](../notes/world-models-agi/README.md). Further reading he links: Barsalou [Perceptual Symbol Systems](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11301525/), his own [cognitive programs](https://www.science.org/doi/abs/10.1126/scirobotics.aav3150) and [From CAPTCHA to commonsense](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fncom.2020.554097/full).

## 2026-09-09 - [Harness Engineering — a 21-paper genealogy](https://academy.dair.ai/papers/collections/harness-engineering) (DAIR.AI, from YC Paper Club: Harness Edition, 2026-08-26)
A curated reading list that defines a **harness** as *everything between the model weights and the world* — the loop, the context it assembles, the tools/skills it can reach, the sub-agents it can spawn, and lately the harness code itself. The spine of the list is one claim we care about a lot: **the same weight file scores 30% or 95% on the same benchmark depending only on what surrounds it** (their running example: Prime Agent takes ARC-AGI-3 from 30% → 95.5% on identical weights). It's organized as a genealogy in five acts — read-in-order, each stage "gives the loop something new it is allowed to do":

1. **The V0 harness** (in-context only): GPT-2 bare while-not-EOS loop (2019) → few-shot (put examples in the window) → Chain-of-Thought (spend tokens in the output space). "Every harness since budgets tokens rather than calls."
2. **Static harnesses — grow the action space** (fixed harness code, more verbs): WebGPT (reach outside the loop) → Toolformer (tool calling) → **ReAct** (interleave thought+action; their spicy take: models now do this natively, so a modern harness should *stop imposing* it) → Self-Refine (internal evaluator, never leaves the harness) → **Reflexion** (write the env reward back into context as words — seed of the self-improving half) → InterCode (code as the action → tool list stops being finite) → Multi-Agent Collaboration (sub-agents become an *addressable* resource) → **Voyager** (skills: chain→verify→write to a searchable library — "you are reading the design of the SKILLS.md in your repo today") → **MemGPT** (CRUD over its own context = transcript becomes managed state) → **RLM / Recursive Language Models** (an LLM call inside a REPL issues further LLM calls over slices; context becomes a resource you program against — Prime Agent builds directly on this).
3. **Let the harness learn** (the fixed part stops being fixed): DSPy (optimize the prompt, not hand-write it) → **GEPA** (reflective prompt evolution from natural-language traces, beats RL at a fraction of rollouts) → Darwin Gödel Machine (agents rewrite their own *scaffolding*, archive-of-ancestors, empirical validation replacing the Gödel-machine proof requirement) → Meta-Harness (a coding agent rewrites retrieval/memory/prompt-assembly code around a fixed model; SOTA on Terminal-Bench 2 without touching weights) → Continual Harness (mutate history/memory/skills/prompts/sub-agent specs *while running*, then DAgger-update the weights — "test-time training is the direction that matters most").
4. **Shipped in 2026**: Prime Agent (persistent IPython REPL + recursive addressable sub-agents + continual prompt/skill refinement; the 30%→95.5% result) and OpenJarvis (same argument on-device: 5 primitives, cloud model searches the spec, everything runs locally, ~800× lower marginal cost — harness closes the gap, not the model). Both open source. Plus **QM**, YC's multiplayer agent harness (MIT, no paper — "the brain pulled out of the sandbox and into Postgres").
5. **How you know it worked**: METR's *Measuring AI Ability to Complete Long Software Tasks* (2025) — capability = the *length* of task a system completes, which is what makes harness progress visible as a slope at all.

## 2026-09-08 - [LLM-as-Judge Architectures: Putting Evals Into Your Agent Runtime](https://x.com/josharosen/status/2097324183428444499) (Josh Rosen)
Josh Rosen (X longform article) argues LLM-as-judge is migrating from an *offline* eval tool into the *runtime*, inside the agent loop, on the critical path — so judgment starts to inform control flow (continue / retry / route to a stronger model / gather more evidence / escalate to a human). His framing: deterministic software has schema checks, types, assertions, exact comparison; AI output often can't be checked that way ("is this complete enough to move on? was the answer useful? did the research support the conclusion?"), so where a human would eyeball it, an LLM judge lets the app judge itself. The years of model-eval research on getting models to judge models now transfers one layer up, into application architecture. Six patterns worth stealing, each with real references:
- **Start with another model** — canonical judge gets request+result+rubric+reference, returns score/label/explanation ([LangSmith], [Phoenix], [DeepEval]). Key point: the judge needn't be *smarter* — judging a narrow property (is every claim supported by the evidence?) is far easier than doing the work, which is why specialized/cheap judge models exist ([Prometheus](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.248/), Galileo). At runtime volumes the economics push toward small distilled evaluators.
- **Break the judgment apart** — decompose "was this good?" into small well-prompted judges (addresses request? claims supported? work completed?) combined with structure. [G-Eval](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.153/) (model generates eval steps from criteria first); DeepEval's DAG-based eval = LLM judgments inside a deterministic decision graph.
- **Compare instead of score** — models are bad at "7 vs 8", good at "A or B". Pairwise judging (+ randomize order for position bias). Runtime use: agent generates several plans, judge picks; or two agents analyze independently and a third compares before committing an action.
- **Judge the work, not the answer** — for a 20-min agent the final result hides wrong-doc retrieval, wrong tool, lucky wandering. Move judges *into the trace* at the checkpoints where real decisions happen (source selection before synthesis; proposed approach before implementation; evidence before an operational action). Phoenix already ships evaluators for retrieval relevance / tool selection / tool invocation / tool responses.
- **Use more than one judge** — panels/personas/voting/debate ([MAJ-EVAL](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.790/)). The sharp insight: even if you don't care about a 2–1 vote, you care *a lot* that judges **disagree** — agreement raises confidence, disagreement is your signal to retry with a stronger model, gather evidence, or route to a human.
- **Judge the judge** — the judge is still an LLM with the same failure modes: position bias, style/length bias, self-preference (favoring its own outputs), struggling on near-ties. Benchmarks: [LLMBar](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/LLMBar), [JudgeBench](https://mlanthology.org/iclr/2025/tan2025iclr-judgebench/), [RewardBench](https://github.com/allenai/reward-bench). Anthropic's [Bloom](https://www.anthropic.com/research/bloom) vetted candidate judges against human-labeled transcripts + a meta-judge; the cheap application version = periodically compare judge vs humans on the decisions that matter, and move the rubric/model/boundary when disagreement is unacceptable.
- **Put determinism around the judge** — don't turn every decision into another LLM call ("throw more agents at more agents"). If it can be checked deterministically (tests, schema, DB check, policy engine), do that; reserve the LLM judge for the genuinely fuzzy part. [OpenAI's grader architecture](https://platform.openai.com/docs/api-reference/graders) mirrors this: model-based graders *alongside* deterministic string/Python graders, combinable. Drawing that deterministic/non-deterministic boundary well is the biggest value-add at the application layer.

**Implication + the honest cost:** putting judges on the critical path means judge mistakes become *application* failures. A slightly-noisy offline judge is annoying; the same judge in front of every important action can loop, block good work, approve bad work, and add latency everywhere. He's bullish but explicitly says these architectures need to mature before we're comfortable with judges on the production critical path.

Relevance to us: this is the *soft*-verification complement to `notes/verification/` (formal/deterministic vericoding) and `notes/prompt-enforcement/` (deterministic instruction enforcement). The recurring lesson lines up with Bargury's hard-vs-soft-boundary post already in this file — an LLM judge is a **soft** boundary, so wrap it in **deterministic** checks and use judge *disagreement* as an escalation signal rather than trusting a lone judge. Directly useful for OpenHands work: a critic/judge in the agent loop (cf. our condenser/critic paths) should be decomposed, checkpoint-placed on the trace, and fenced by deterministic graders. It's a blog synthesis, not a paper — but it's a clean map of the judge-in-the-loop design space with good primary links, so it lives here rather than as a `notes/` entry. Also connects to `misc/llm-evaluators-recognize-and-favor-their-own-generations.md` (the self-preference bias he names under "Judge the judge").

[LangSmith]: https://docs.langchain.com/langsmith/evaluation
[Phoenix]: https://arize.com/docs/phoenix/
[DeepEval]: https://deepeval.com/
