1   Introduction
Real-world document questions often require evidence scattered across large, evolving document collections.
Corpus-level long-context reasoning requires agents to locate and integrate this evidence even when the
collection exceeds their context windows. Longer windows expand the material available within a single
invocation (Gemini Team, 2024; Yang et al., 2025b; Shang et al., 2025), but models remain sensitive to
evidence position and distractors even when the relevant content fits (Liu et al., 2024; Hsieh et al., 2024;
Ling et al., 2025; Zhuang et al., 2025a). Agents must therefore search selectively, read relevant passages, and
accumulate evidence across multiple interactions. As these interactions unfold, the challenge extends from
finding evidence to keeping it usable, revising it when needed, and carrying it into the final decision.
We view this process at three levels. Evidence retrieval (Level 1) locates relevant passages while filtering
distractors. Evidence editing (Level 2) selects, reconciles, and revises retrieved content. Evidence transformation
(Level 3) turns the resulting evidence into an answer. Training methods supervise retrieval trajectories and
memory edits (Lin et al., 2026b; Chen et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2025; Gao and Marttinen, 2026), while agent
harnesses enable adaptive document access and persistent storage (Cao et al., 2026; Chen et al., 2023; Lee
et al., 2024; Zhao et al., 2024; Packer et al., 2024; Karten et al., 2026; Yu et al., 2026). These advances
strengthen evidence acquisition and maintenance, yet the transition from accumulated evidence to an evolving
conclusion often remains implicit. The conclusions that determine what to search for next and what answer
to produce remain embedded in session history, free-form notes, or transient reasoning.
At corpus scale, this implicit transition can lead to two coupled failure modes. (i) Unbounded exploration
occurs when retrieval continues without resolving decision-relevant information gaps, potentially exhausting
the interaction budget. (ii) Evidence-state drift occurs when previously acquired evidence or later corrections
become disconnected from subsequent reasoning, leaving the final decision based on stale or incomplete
evidence. These failures can reinforce each other as drift obscures which information needs have already been
resolved, prompting further retrieval that expands the history and makes relevant evidence harder to recover.
We introduce LongHarness, a model-agnostic harness that maintains source-linked evidence and evolving
conclusions as coupled persistent states. Unresolved conclusion gaps guide subsequent search and reading,
while accepted evidence informs revisions to the conclusions and their remaining gaps. Its core mechanism,
Evidence–Conclusion Coevolution, uses unresolved conclusions to guide exploration toward missing information,
while newly acquired evidence strengthens or revises the conclusions that directed the search. By making the
remaining information needs explicit, this feedback directs retrieval toward gaps that matter to the decision
and helps curb unbounded exploration. LongHarness couples this loop with Evidence Lifecycle Management,
which keeps accumulated evidence current and traceable throughout the interaction. The EvidenceBank
retains source-linked statements and tracks which remain active throughout the evidence lifecycle, helping
reduce evidence-state drift by keeping the basis for subsequent reasoning aligned with the latest findings.
We evaluate LongHarness on OfficeQA Pro V1 and V2, AA-LCR, and the LongBench v2 Long subset
using GLM-5.3-Flash and Qwen3.8-Flash. OfficeQA Pro requires reasoning over roughly 100M tokens in V1,
while V2 doubles the corpus and introduces more demanding compositional questions. Compared with direct
reasoning using the same backbone, LongHarness raises GLM-5.3-Flash exact match from 15.8% to 60.2%
on V1 and from 4.4% to 61.1% on V2. Against state-of-the-art agent harnesses, it surpasses Claude Code by
12.0 percentage points on V1 and Codex by 18.9 points on V2. With Qwen3.8-Flash, it reaches 61.7% on V1,
outperforming Claude Code by 8.3 points and Codex by 12.0 points. Trajectory analysis with GLM-5.3-Flash
further connects these gains to evidence management, with V2 success rates exceeding the stronger of Claude
Code and Codex at each stage by 11.1 points in retrieval, 2.7 in evidence editing, and 9.3 in transformation.
Our contributions are summarized as follows.

• We formulate corpus-level long-context reasoning as evidence lifecycle management, viewing the construction
  and continual refinement of the evidence supporting a decision as the central reasoning process. This
  perspective unifies evidence retrieval, editing, and transformation through the evolving role of evidence in
  decision-making.

• We develop LongHarness, a model-agnostic harness that operationalizes this formulation through Evidence–
  Conclusion Coevolution and Evidence Lifecycle Management. It maintains evidence and conclusions as

acquires evidence through an external environment ED under a resource budget. Its state St = (Et , Ct , Ht )
comprises source-linked evidence, evidence-linked conclusions and unresolved gaps, and prior reasoning, actions,
and observations. Given q, document map MD , state St , and remaining budget Bt , the policy selects an
action and an optional conclusion update, and the environment returns the next state and observation.

LongHarness couples two mechanisms (Figure 2). Evidence–Conclusion Coevolution uses unresolved
conclusions to guide evidence acquisition and accepted evidence to strengthen or revise conclusions (Section 2.2).

preserving source references (Section 2.3). A shared interface supports adaptive interleaving of these operations
with document access, computation, and final submission (Section 2.4).

2.2    Evidence–Conclusion Coevolution
Separating evidence from inference. The EvidenceBank maintains source-linked statements extracted from
passages the agent has read and serves as the persistent state in which evidence is admitted and revised,
while the Conclusion State records the agent’s current interpretation of that evidence through established
claims, supported hypotheses, and unresolved gaps. Maintaining these representations separately distinguishes
the acquired evidence from the inferences drawn from it, while explicit references connect each claim to its
supporting evidence. Each unresolved gap identifies information needed to establish, qualify, or revise the
current conclusion. At the level of a reasoning cycle, the two states coevolve through conclusion-guided
evidence acquisition and evidence-grounded conclusion revision,

references and unresolved gaps, keeping the Conclusion State aligned with the current evidence. Before
final submission, the agent incorporates any relevant evidence not yet reflected in this state and checks whether
the remaining gaps could change the requested decision.

2.3        Evidence Lifecycle Management
Evidence Lifecycle Management governs how source-linked statements enter, persist in, and leave the active
evidence state in the EvidenceBank. A statement begins as a candidate extracted from a passage the
agent has read, becomes active upon admission, and may subsequently be revised or retired as new evidence
arrives. This lifecycle requires (i) traceability to observed source passages, (ii) persistent availability across
interactions, and (iii) explicit updates when a statement’s supporting evidence changes.
The EvidenceBank implements these requirements through source-linked admission, persistent storage, and
explicit revision. For traceability, each candidate statement must cite source references registered by prior read
actions, with the controller checking reference validity and the agent judging whether the statement faithfully
represents its sources. For persistence, admitted statements remain available in the EvidenceBank across
subsequent interactions, allowing the agent to reuse them without reconstructing the evidence from earlier
observations. For explicit revision, a corrected statement is admitted as a new active entry and the previous
entry is marked as superseded, while a retracted statement leaves the active state without a replacement. The

agent observes these EvidenceBank updates before revising the affected conclusions, and final references
resolve to active statements and their underlying source passages.

2.4   Agent–Environment Interaction

At each turn, the policy selects an action at and an optional conclusion update C  bt under the formulation in
Section 2.1. The environment executes the action and returns an observation, while the harness enforces the
interaction protocol, source-reference constraints, and resource limits. The shared action space lets the policy
interleave evidence acquisition, maintenance, and use. Search locates candidate documents or passages, and
Read returns selected text and registers source references. The agent proposes source-linked evidence through
upsert and corrects or retracts entries through revise (Section 2.3). Compute evaluates numerical expressions
supplied by the policy and validates their declared evidence references, and Final submits the answer with
supporting references.
EvidenceBank mutations defer conclusion updates until the agent observes their results. The policy
then revises affected claims and references through optional partial updates, with omitted fields retained.
Superseding or retracting evidence does not automatically rewrite dependent conclusions. The controller
checks declared evidence references at computation and final submission, while the policy remains responsible
for source fidelity and whether the evidence supports its conclusions. Reasoning, actions, and observations
accumulate in the history, while evidence and conclusions persist across turns. Final acceptance requires a
valid answer structure and references resolving to active evidence and source passages. Resource exhaustion
or runtime failure may terminate the episode without an accepted answer. Algorithm 1 in Appendix B.5
summarizes the procedure.


3     Experiments

3.1   Experimental Setup
Benchmarks. We evaluate on OfficeQA Pro V1 and V2 (Opsahl-Ong et al., 2026), AA-LCR (Artificial
Analysis Team, 2026), and the Long subset of LongBench v2 (Bai et al., 2024), covering both corpus-level
reasoning and reasoning over provided contexts. OfficeQA Pro V1 contains 697 Treasury documents totaling
approximately 100M tokens, while V2 expands the corpus to 1,435 documents and introduces composite
statistical questions that require combining values from multiple sources. Agents receive access to the full
corpus without question-specific gold documents, making source discovery part of the task. AA-LCR provides
the relevant context for each of its 100 questions, while the LongBench v2 Long subset evaluates reasoning
over the benchmark’s longest contexts. We report exact match and accuracy within 5% relative error on
OfficeQA Pro, and Exact accuracy on AA-LCR and LongBench v2.

Models and harnesses. Our direct-reasoning baselines include Qwen3.8-27B, Qwen3.7-Max, and Qwen3.8-
Flash (Yang et al., 2025a); DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731 and DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813 (DeepSeek-AI et al., 2026);
Gemini-3.8-Flash (Google DeepMind, 2026); GPT-5.6-Luna and GPT-5.6-Sol (Singh et al., 2026); GLM-5.3
and GLM-5.3-Flash (GLM-5-Team et al., 2026); and Kimi-K3 (Kimi Team et al., 2026). Citations refer to
the corresponding model families or release-specific model cards. On OfficeQA Pro, these models receive
question-relevant passages retrieved with BM25 into a 256K-token context window, while LongBench v2
contexts follow the official truncation procedure with the same token limit. We compare Codex (OpenAI,
2026), Claude Code (Anthropic, 2026), RLM (Zhang et al., 2025), and LongHarness using GLM-5.3-Flash
and Qwen3.8-Flash as matched backbones, holding model identity fixed within each comparison. All harnesses
receive the same questions and document corpus without access to reference answers or gold source annotations.

Implementation details. All backbone models use temperature 1, top-p 0.95, maximum reasoning effort,
and an output limit of 32,768 tokens per request. Codex and Claude Code each allow up to 200 model
turns and 200 tool calls, while RLM allows 30 iterations and LongHarness allows 200 steps subject to
a cumulative read budget of 1,000,000 tokens and a limit of 60 search calls. We grade AA-LCR harness
responses with GPT-5.6-Luna. Due to computational resource constraints, the main comparison uses one run

3.2     Main Results
Table 1 shows that direct baselines reach at most 23.2% exact match on OfficeQA Pro V1 and 5.6% on V2
despite receiving question-relevant passages in a 256K-token window. At corpus scale, this fixed window
cannot accommodate all potentially relevant evidence, and passages selected before reasoning may omit
information needed to resolve intermediate conclusions. LongHarness uses these conclusions to guide further
retrieval and accumulates the resulting evidence across interactions, raising GLM-5.3-Flash exact match from
15.8% to 60.2% on V1 and from 4.4% to 61.1% on V2.
With GLM-5.3-Flash, LongHarness outperforms the strongest competing harness by 12.0 percentage points
on OfficeQA Pro V1 and 18.9 points on V2. The larger advantage on V2 is consistent with maintaining
evidence across interactions as more scattered values must be located, reconciled, and combined. With
Qwen3.8-Flash, LongHarness also leads on both versions, reaching 61.7% on V1 and 51.1% on V2. Its
advantage over the strongest competing harness is 8.3 points on V1 but narrows to 1.1 points over Codex on
V2, showing that the size of the gain varies with the backbone and evaluation setting. On the LongBench v2
Long subset, LongHarness reaches 75.9% with GLM-5.3-Flash and 76.9% with Qwen3.8-Flash, exceeding
the next-best harness by 6.5 and 7.4 percentage points, respectively. On AA-LCR, it scores 84.0% and 87.0%,
leading the strongest competing harness by 5.0 and 4.0 points. LongHarness achieves the highest average
Exact score across the four evaluation settings with both backbones, reaching 70.3% and 69.2%, respectively.

3.3   Ablation Study
We ask whether evidence–conclusion coevolution and evidence lifecycle management are critical to the
effectiveness of LongHarness, and examine this question through five ablations in Table 2. (i) w/o Persistent
States removes the EvidenceBank and Conclusion State while retaining interaction history. (ii) w/o
Evidence–Conclusion Coevolution retains the EvidenceBank but removes the Conclusion State and its
retrieval feedback. (iii) Naive Evidence Accumulation preserves conclusion feedback but permits only append-
only evidence updates. (iv) w/o Explicit Conclusion-Guided Exploration retains both states but removes the
instructions to guide retrieval using conclusion gaps. (v) Generic Working Notes retains the EvidenceBank
and replaces structured conclusions with editable free-text notes that can record conclusions, unresolved
questions, and evidence references.
Removing both persistent states reduces exact match by 4.5 percentage points on V1 and 7.8 on V2, showing
that explicit evidence and conclusion states contribute beyond access to the interaction history. Removing the
maintained Conclusion State and its retrieval feedback reduces accuracy by 5.3 and 9.4 points. Restricting
the EvidenceBank to append-only accumulation yields 56.4% on V1 and 51.1% on V2, corresponding
to decreases of 3.8 and 10.0 points. These results support the value of revising and retiring evidence as
new observations arrive, even when conclusion feedback is preserved. Removing the explicit conclusion-
guided exploration instructions while retaining both persistent states reduces accuracy by 4.5 and 7.8 points,
supporting the value of instructing the policy to use conclusion gaps to guide retrieval beyond making those
states available. Replacing structured conclusions with generic working notes reduces accuracy by 6.8 and 7.8
points, showing that the full design outperforms editable free-text notes that can also retain conclusions, open
questions, and evidence references. All five ablations incur larger losses on V2, consistent with the greater
importance of maintaining and reconciling evidence when questions require combining values from multiple
sources.

3.4                       Interaction Length and Trajectory Analysis
We ask where agent harnesses fail across evidence retrieval, editing, and transformation, and how these failures
change from OfficeQA Pro V1 to V2. For each harness, we use GPT-5.6-Luna at temperature 0 to audit
incorrect episodes and assess evidence retrieval, evidence adoption, and answer transformation. Exact-correct
episodes are counted as passing the first two stages. Let E1 denote the presence of required evidence in the
trajectory, E2 its retention and reconciliation for use in the final decision, and E3 an exactly correct answer.
We measure retrieval coverage and conditional editing and transformation success as
                                                               r1 = P (E1 ),                           r2 = P (E2 | E1 ),                   r3 = P (E3 | E1 , E2 ).                                                     (5)

Table 3 shows that LongHarness matches Claude Code’s retrieval coverage on V1 and achieves higher
conditional evidence-adoption and answer-conversion rates. On V2, its retrieval, evidence-adoption, and
answer-conversion rates are 87.8%, 87.3%, and 79.7%, respectively. The conditional adoption and conversion
rates remain close to their V1 values of 87.6% and 80.8%, indicating similar evidence-use outcomes across the
two evaluated settings. The conditional rates are computed over the questions that pass the preceding stages
for each harness and describe stage outcomes within the analyzed runs.

LongHarness records unresolved information needs in the Conclusion State and retains acquired evidence
in the EvidenceBank, allowing subsequent retrieval to focus on missing information while reusing values
already established. As questions require more cross-source checks, this organization helps avoid repeatedly
searching for or re-establishing the same facts. This offers a plausible explanation for the smaller shift in

4     Related Work

4.1   Long-Context Reasoning
Post-training supervises evidence acquisition and use. LongRLVR rewards correct identification of evidence
chunks (Chen et al., 2026), LongPAS assigns advantages to reasoning steps (Peng et al., 2026), and LongR
measures document consultation through information gain (Ping et al., 2026). Training data include key-fact
chains (Wang et al., 2025), distractor-augmented search trajectories (Lin et al., 2026b), capability-aligned
task mixtures (Lv et al., 2026), and verifiable multi-hop or memory-management tasks (Wan et al., 2025;
Shen et al., 2025). Training also uses agent-generated data and supervision for memory operations. ACC
compiles agent trajectories into long-context QA training examples (Su et al., 2026), AgenticLU distills
self-generated clarification trajectories without external teachers (Zhuang et al., 2025b), MAVEN rewards
evidence-memory edits (Gao and Marttinen, 2026), and Memory-R2 credits individual memory operations
(Yan et al., 2026). LongHarness instead couples evidence and conclusions at runtime, linking retrieval,
editing, and transformation without additional policy training.

4.2   Agentic Harnesses
ReAct couples reasoning with action (Yao et al., 2023), recursive language models process context under
program control (Zhang et al., 2025), and Prime Agent maintains persistent REPL state (Karten et al., 2026).
Codex and Claude Code support long-context reasoning (Cao et al., 2026); other harnesses develop bounded
long-horizon decomposition, harness maintenance, and persistent memory (Zheng et al., 2026; Lin et al., 2026a;
Packer et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2026; Zhang et al., 2026; Pan et al., 2026). MemWalker uses memory trees (Chen
et al., 2023), ReadAgent uses gist representations (Lee et al., 2024), GraphReader uses chunk graphs (Li et al.,
2024), and multi-agent systems coordinate readers (Zhao et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2024). SLIDERS reconciles
evidence under an induced schema (Joshi et al., 2026), ReContext replays found-but-unused evidence (Zhao
et al., 2026), and TRACE evaluates tool-augmented reasoning trajectories (Kim et al., 2026). LedgerMind
uses grounding checks and event-triggered repair to address unsupported or inconsistent claims (Du et al.,
2026). However, existing claims can remain grounded while the evidence needed to answer the question is
still incomplete. In contrast, LongHarness focuses on corpus-level long-context reasoning, maintaining
source-linked evidence alongside evolving conclusions and unresolved information needs. These needs guide
subsequent search and reading, while newly acquired evidence informs revisions to conclusions and remaining
gaps.


5     Conclusion
We propose LongHarness, a model-agnostic harness that unifies evidence retrieval, editing, and transfor-
mation for corpus-level long-context reasoning. Evidence–Conclusion Coevolution couples exploration with
conclusion revision, while Evidence Lifecycle Management keeps evidence current and traceable. Evaluations
on OfficeQA Pro, AA-LCR, and LongBench v2 show gains with two backbones. With GLM-5.3-Flash,
LongHarness exceeds Claude Code by 12.0 percentage points on OfficeQA Pro V1 and Codex by 18.9
points on V2. Ablations support the complementary roles of persistent states, conclusion-guided retrieval,

and evidence revision. Trajectory analysis shows similar conditional evidence-use rates across V1 and V2 and
a smaller increase in interaction length for LongHarness than for the competing harnesses.

A     Benchmark Details
Table 4 summarizes the four evaluation settings, covering external-corpus retrieval in OfficeQA Pro and
reasoning over provided contexts in AA-LCR and LongBench v2.

OfficeQA Pro. OfficeQA Pro V1 (Opsahl-Ong et al., 2026) contains 133 questions over 697 Treasury
documents totaling approximately 100M tokens, while V2 contains 90 questions over an expanded corpus of
1,435 documents. V2 places greater emphasis on composite numerical questions requiring values from different
series, versions, or documents. Both versions expose the full corpus without question-specific gold documents,
requiring the system to identify the sources needed for each answer. We report exact match, which requires
the requested value and format, and accuracy within 5% relative error.

AA-LCR-1.1. We use AA-LCR-1.1 (Artificial Analysis Team, 2026),1 which contains 100 questions
associated with 30 document sets comprising 234 documents in total, with an average of 99,325 tokens per set.
The documents include company and industry reports, government consultations, academic papers, legal and
marketing materials, and survey reports. Questions require synthesis, temporal reasoning, conditional logic,
or numerical computation across documents rather than direct retrieval from a single passage. Each question
supplies its relevant document set, allowing the evaluation to focus on evidence use within the provided
context. For direct-reasoning models, we report the AA-LCR scores published by Artificial Analysis in its
official evaluation results.

LongBench v2. LongBench v2 (Bai et al., 2024) contains 503 four-option questions covering single-document
QA, multi-document QA, long in-context learning, long-dialogue history understanding, code-repository
understanding, and long structured-data understanding. The full benchmark spans contexts of 8K–2M words,
most below 128K words. We evaluate the official Long subset of 108 questions, whose contexts in the release
used here range from approximately 132K to 1.38M words with a median of 204K words. This subset contains
29 code-repository understanding questions, 26 long in-context learning questions, 24 single-document QA
questions, 23 multi-document QA questions, and 6 structured-data understanding questions. Each question
provides a long context and four answer options, and performance is measured by exact accuracy on the
selected option.

B.2      EvidenceBank Operations
An upsert validates each proposed statement independently by checking that its source references were
registered by earlier read actions, so a batch may be partially accepted. The controller validates reference
registration, while the policy judges whether the statement faithfully represents its sources.
Accepted EvidenceBank entries retain immutable identifiers, and corrections are recorded through revise
rather than in-place edits. A non-null replacement creates a corrected active entry and marks the previous
entry as superseded, while a null replacement retracts the previous entry without creating a replacement.
Superseded and retracted entries remain in the revision record but cannot be cited in compute or final requests.
Existing conclusions may retain their references until the policy updates them.

B.3      State Contract
The Conclusion State contains established claims and supported hypotheses linked to active EvidenceBank
entries, together with unresolved gaps describing information that could change those conclusions. This
representation separates the agent’s current inferences from the source-linked statements maintained in the
EvidenceBank.
When an action updates the EvidenceBank, conclusion revision follows observation of the update result
so that claims reference accepted evidence. For other actions, the proposed conclusion update is merged
before execution. The complete interaction history remains available alongside the persistent evidence and
conclusion states throughout the episode, and final supporting references must resolve to active EvidenceBank
entries and their underlying source text. Conclusion updates replace the supplied lists of established claims,
hypotheses, or open gaps. Omitted lists remain unchanged. When evidence becomes inactive, transition logs
flag affected conclusion references, but the runtime does not automatically delete or rewrite those claims or
block subsequent search and reading. Compute and final validate the evidence IDs declared in their requests,
rather than all references retained in the Conclusion State. Compute also does not establish that literal
operands match the cited source values.

B.4   Runtime Settings
At each step, the policy receives the system instructions and task specification, followed by the complete
interaction history and the current runtime state. The history includes all previous reasoning, actions, and tool
observations. Historical observations are retained in full rather than replaced with summaries or placeholders.
The runtime state presents the EvidenceBank, Conclusion State, and remaining resource budgets. These
explicit states provide an organized record of acquired evidence and unresolved information needs alongside the
full interaction history. The cumulative read budget counts only source text returned by the read operation
across the episode.
The cumulative reading budget limits source acquisition over the episode; each model request is separately
subject to the endpoint’s context limit. The client does not automatically truncate or compact the interaction
history. A request failure that persists after the model client’s retry handling, including rejection of an
oversized request, is recorded as model_request_failed and terminates the episode. Episodes that terminate
without an accepted final answer are scored as incorrect.
The same state and action contract applies across benchmarks, with OfficeQA Pro exposing the full external
corpus and AA-LCR and LongBench v2 exposing each question’s provided context as the available document
collection. Reference answers, gold source annotations, and question-specific document labels are withheld
from the agent. All backbone models use temperature 1, top-p 0.95, maximum reasoning effort, and an output
limit of 32,768 tokens per request.

C     Experimental Protocol Details

C.1   Benchmark Preparation
OfficeQA Pro exposes the complete V1 or V2 corpus as an external document environment, with a document
map containing identifiers, titles, and section structure. Agents must select relevant sources from this
corpus without access to question-specific gold documents. For OfficeQA Pro V1 and V2, we use the Parsed
Documents provided by the benchmark authors.
AA-LCR-1.1 retains the document set supplied with each question, including its official grouping and document
order. Direct-reasoning baselines receive these documents in the prompt, while agent harnesses access the
same collection through search and bounded reading. For LongBench v2, direct-reasoning baselines follow the
official truncation procedure with a 256K-token limit, while harnesses access the original Long-subset context
through the external document interface.
Reference answers, gold evidence annotations, and question-specific document labels are excluded from
agent-facing inputs, and document filtering, ranking, and truncation do not use answer information or the
final prediction.

Document structure and retrieval. LongHarness constructs its document environment from the
benchmark text, preserving source character offsets for subsequent evidence references. Existing section
annotations are retained. Otherwise, sections are identified from Markdown headings, with a single root
section used for documents without headings. Searchable blocks follow paragraph boundaries, with oversized
paragraphs split into non-overlapping blocks of at most 1,024 estimated tokens, preferably at newline
boundaries. Token estimates count word sequences and individual non-whitespace punctuation symbols.

The search interface supports lexical and regular-expression queries, optionally restricted to specified documents
or sections. Lexical queries use BM25-style ranking over the text blocks. Search results provide source locators
and short snippets, which guide subsequent read operations. Read operations return source text together
with registered source-span identifiers, allowing EvidenceBank entries to refer back to the corresponding
document offsets.

C.2   Harness Configuration
All harnesses operate in an isolated workspace containing the question, available documents, and an output
directory. Tool-level network access is disabled, and file access is confined to the workspace, excluding reference
answers, scoring scripts, and external run metadata.
We use Codex CLI version 0.135.0 and Claude Code version 2.1.216. Claude Code and Codex inspect and
modify workspace files through their native tools and local shell commands. RLM accesses documents
programmatically through its Python REPL. LongHarness accesses documents through its defined search
and reading operations and maintains evidence and conclusion states through explicit update actions. Table 8
summarizes these interfaces and resource limits. Each comparison uses the same questions, available documents,
and backbone model, with the harness interface and execution protocol determining how the model interacts
with the corpus. RLM operates in a local Python REPL whose variables persist across iterations within
an episode. The model can inspect corpus files, process retrieved text programmatically, and invoke model
subcalls through the REPL. The task prompt specifies the question, corpus location, and required answer
format. Our benchmark adapter converts document paths to absolute paths accessible from the REPL and
adds the benchmark-specific output contract. The root loop allows up to 30 iterations. The driver uses the
library’s default maximum recursion depth of one, at which subcalls receive plain model completions rather
than launching further REPL loops. Root and subcalls use the same backbone. If the returned answer does
not satisfy the required output format, the adapter makes one additional call to the same model, instructed
to copy the answer value verbatim into the required wrapper.

Evidence-pipeline audit. We use GPT-5.6-Luna with temperature 0 to assess incorrect episodes using the
recorded reasoning and available tool outputs or evidence-state records. The audit distinguishes failures to
retrieve the required evidence, failures to select or retain that evidence, and failures to transform the adopted
evidence into a correct answer. Answer correctness is determined by the benchmark scorer at tolerance 0;
correct episodes are counted as passing the first two stages by protocol. Retrieval coverage is computed over all
evaluated questions, evidence adoption over questions passing retrieval, and answer conversion over questions
passing both preceding stages. These conditional rates characterize stage outcomes within the analyzed runs.

  You are a ReAct agent for evidence-grounded long-context question answering. Documents remain external to the
       prompt. Investigate them over multiple turns using search, read, compute, EvidenceBank, and final. You decide
       what the evidence means, what remains uncertain, and when the answer is sufficient.

  Follow one revisable loop:

  1. Select the unresolved gap whose resolution is most likely to change the answer.
  2. Use search to locate relevant documents and passages. Search snippets are navigation aids, not citable evidence.
  3. Use read to inspect source text and register source references. Only a source reference returned by read may support
        an EvidenceBank statement.
  4. Save useful source facts as atomic, source-linked EvidenceBank statements. An EvidenceBank identifier becomes
        usable only after a successful EvidenceBank observation.
  5. Only cite EvidenceBank identifiers that were active before the current turn. Evidence created by the current
        EvidenceBank action becomes citable on the next turn.
  6. Revise the Conclusion State when new evidence supports, qualifies, contradicts, or replaces earlier reasoning.

  The EvidenceBank records what the documents state, not whether an answer is correct. Keep statements atomic and
      source-faithful. Retry only rejected statements. If an accepted statement is invalid, use EvidenceBank revise:
      provide a corrected replacement, or use a null replacement to withdraw it.

  For compute, place the source values in one deterministic expression and cite the active EvidenceBank identifiers that
       support those values. After compute, verify direction, unit, and precision. Express the derived result in the
       Conclusion State together with the EvidenceBank identifiers supporting every operand.

  Maintain a compact Conclusion State with three revisable lists:

  - established: conclusions justified by active EvidenceBank entries;
  - supported hypotheses: plausible but unsettled interpretations supported by active EvidenceBank entries;
  - unresolved: concrete missing information that could change the answer.

  Established conclusions and supported hypotheses must cite active EvidenceBank entries. Unsupported possibilities
       belong under unresolved. State the inference, not merely disconnected source facts.

  Search for the missing distinction, not only for the question topic. If retrieval is uninformative, change the query or
       reading strategy instead of repeating it. Read enough context to interpret the source correctly. Before adopting a
       conclusion, verify that its entities, relationships, scope, and requested output match the question. A derived or
       aggregate conclusion must cite every decision-critical premise and use compute when arithmetic is needed.
       Evidence relevance alone is not support.

  Before final, reconstruct the requested answer only from current established conclusions and their sources. Revise stale
       conclusions. Check a plausible alternative only when it could change the answer. Continue only while resolving a
       remaining unresolved gap could change the result. Do not search merely because unrelated uncertainty remains.

  Place the answer-bearing conclusion under established. Submit final with its active EvidenceBank identifiers. Follow the
       requested choice label, units, precision, ordering, and output format.

  Invoke exactly one operation per turn. Conclusion State updates may be partial, and omitted lists retain their previous
       values. Evidence mutation turns defer Conclusion State updates until their observations have been seen. Use final
       as the only way to submit the answer.

E    Case Study
Figure 4 compares GLM-5.3-Flash trajectories for computing the geometric mean of 80 monthly Treasury
expenditures in OfficeQA Pro V1. We randomly selected this case from Codex trajectories that failed
the exact-match criterion. Steps in Figure 4 denote shell-command calls for Codex and policy actions for
LongHarness. Codex locates the relevant table within 22 command calls but leaves missing values implicit,
later making 103 consecutive backward reads (unbounded exploration). It computes 4958.09 using 4100 for May
1942, then recognizes the corrected value 4400 but retains the earlier result in its final answer. Replacing this
operand yields the reference answer 4962.46. Codex therefore fails exact match but passes the 5% tolerance
criterion, with a relative error of approximately 0.088%. This evidence-state drift illustrates a recognized
correction that was not incorporated into the final computation.
After recording 4400 in the EvidenceBank, LongHarness tracks cross-issue consistency as an unresolved
Conclusion State gap. Further reading corroborates the value and corrects two other entries. Evidence
and conclusions incorporate these revisions before computation. LongHarness returns 4962.46 in 10 policy
actions, showing how conclusion-guided retrieval and evidence maintenance carry corrections into the final
decision.
