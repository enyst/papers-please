- **One-line take:** The editorial that maps the whole issue — its thesis is that "world model" is not one thing but a family (causal, self-referential, goal-directed, collective, narrative), and the open question for AI is whether scale-trained systems acquire the *value-laden, temporally-embedded, embodied* kind of modelling that biological minds run on, or only surface statistics.

- **Why it's the anchor:** Read this first. It names the axes the other 17 papers sit on, and it takes the "life–mind continuity" stance seriously — that mind is continuous with life, so world-modelling should be studied across substrates (cells, brains, economies, machines), not just in humans and LLMs.

- **The three groupings it uses** (useful as the issue's own taxonomy):
  1. **Approaches to analysing emergent intelligence** — complex-systems/emergence lens, levels-of-analysis (Marr), representation complexity of model-based vs model-free RL.
  2. **Principles of intelligence and world-modelling systems** — goals/telic states, causal learning + empowerment, self-modelling, good-regulator theorem, grounding/language.
  3. **Collective & diverse world modelling** — economies as cognitive glue, Rogers' paradox with AI, consciousness in unconventional embodiments, topological limits on self-organization.

- **Recurring tension (the spine of the issue):** impressive LLM capability vs. whether it is *grounded understanding*. The editors keep returning to embodiment, temporality, and value/goals as the things current AI may be missing — not as a settled verdict but as the productive fault line.

- **Why it matters for us (SmolPaws / Engel):** this is the conceptual map for the exact question Engel cares about — concept formation and whether statistical learners *understand*. The "hard problem = the problem problem" (see the AI-for-science note) and "grounding via goals/empowerment" threads are the ones most relevant to agent memory + concept formation.

- **Notable author list:** Levin (diverse intelligence), Mitchell & Krakauer (understanding), Tenenbaum & Gershman (theory-based learning), David Ha (world models), Rish (scaling). It's a deliberate cross-disciplinary cast, which is the point.

- **Abstract:** This special issue examines how natural and artificial intelligences model the world, and what this reveals about cognition and relationships between life and mind. Rather than adopting a single definition, it considers how world models function and emerge in biological and artificial systems — causal, self-referential, goal-directed, collective and narrative forms. A recurring theme is the extent to which current AI trained on vast data learns the context-sensitive, temporally embedded, value-laden dimensions of world modelling that characterize biological intelligences, or whether capabilities arise primarily from statistical surface regularities. Contributions also raise embodiment, complexity, learning architectures, and the social/scientific contexts of world models, aiming to clarify the conceptual landscape and outline questions for future research on grounded understanding, robust agency, and human-like general intelligence.
