- **One-line take:** "All intelligence is collective intelligence" — parts aligning to a system-level goal. Uses statistical mechanics (domain-wall free-energy scaling across the Potts model, autoregressive models, and hierarchical networks) to derive *topological* conditions under which local interactions can or cannot sustain global order.

- **The intriguing payoff:** the same combinatorics-of-interactions argument explains why multiscale biological systems organize into complex patterns, while **rudimentary language models are challenged by long output sequences** — i.e. a physics-of-ordering reason autoregressive models struggle to maintain long-range coherence. That's a surprisingly concrete bridge from spin models to LLM long-context failure.

- **Abstract:** All intelligence is collective, made of parts that align to system-level goals. Considering a system on the vertices of a planar graph with pairwise edge interactions, such systems can exhibit long-range order distinguishing phases of macroscopic behaviour; spontaneous ordering models neural and basal cognition. The authors discuss necessary topological conditions for an ordered phase, studying free-energy scaling under domain-wall formation in three models (Potts, autoregressive, hierarchical networks) to show how interaction combinatorics prevent or allow spontaneous ordering — explaining why multiscale biological systems organize into complex patterns whereas rudimentary language models are challenged by long output sequences.
