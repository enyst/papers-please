- **One-line take:** Dusts off Conant & Ashby's Every Good Regulator Theorem — "every good regulator of a system must be a model of that system" — and recasts it as a principled account of why an intelligent controller ends up holding a compressed world model. Old cybernetics, re-read for the world-model era.

- **Why it's worth logging:** it connects the world-model discourse to control theory's oldest formal result, and pushes to second-order cybernetics (an internal model M observing and supervising the system–regulator loop). It also flags where tight "good regulation" breaks down — non-uniform, out-of-distribution phenomena — via a statistical-mechanics recasting (temporal criticality, non-normal denoising).

- **Status:** abstract-only — control-theory framing, tangential to our agent-memory/concept-formation core, so logged at abstract depth. **Flag:** full read if the "controller ⇒ model" formalism becomes relevant to agent architecture.

- **Abstract:** The Every Good Regulator Theorem (EGRT) identifies conditions under which a regulator can match a system's dynamical behaviour. Recast in a modern context, it gives insight into how autonomous learning systems might use a compressed global representation (world model): one-to-one regulator↔system mappings preserve useful variety to match all system outcomes. The EGRT extends to second-order cybernetics, where an internal model observes the system and supervises a closed-loop mapping. The authors also show physical phenomena (temporal criticality, non-normal denoising, alternating procedural acquisition) recast behaviour as statistical mechanics, challenging tightly coupled good regulation for out-of-distribution phenomena, aiming to recast the EGRT for developing adaptive world models.
