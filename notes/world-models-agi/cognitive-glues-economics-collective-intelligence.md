- **One-line take:** Identifies the **price system** as the "cognitive glue" of the economy-as-collective-intelligence — a shared model of relative scarcities that lets autonomous agents form mutually compatible plans. Bold generalization: *any* cognitive glue (what binds parts into a collective mind) must solve the same coordination problem the way prices do, making the price system a template for all cognitive glues.

- **Why it's worth logging:** it's the issue's clearest "shared world model as coordination substrate" argument, via the TAME (Technological Approach to Mind Everywhere) framework. The "cognitive glue = shared model of relative scarcities" abstraction is a memorable lens on multi-agent coordination.

- **Status:** abstract-only — economics/collective-intelligence framing, tangential to our core, logged for completeness. **Flag:** full read if multi-agent coordination / shared-world-model design becomes a focus.

- **Abstract:** Collective intelligence has emergent problem-solving capacities differing from its subunits'; multi-scale systems make it imperative to understand interaction policies sufficient for emergence of collective intelligences. The economy is a complex adaptive system exemplifying collective intelligence; the authors identify the price system as its cognitive glue — a coordinating affordance enabling mutually compatible plans. Using the TAME framework, they argue any cognitive glue must solve the same problem the price system solves in broadly the same way, making it a generic template for cognitive glues, and propose research combining biology and economics.
