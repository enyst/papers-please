- **One-line take:** Challenges the standard RL split of world model into state-representation (descriptive) + reward-function (prescriptive), proposing instead that both **co-emerge from the agent's goal**. Formalizes "telic states" = classes of goal-equivalent experience distributions, with goal-directed learning as statistical divergence between behavioural policies and desirable experience features. Draws on Buddhist epistemology.

- **Why it's interesting:** it's a genuinely different computational stance on the descriptive/normative relationship — instead of "learn the world, then learn what's rewarding in it," the value structure and the state structure are two faces of the same goal-shaped experience distribution. Claims to unify behavioural, phenomenological, and neural dimensions of purposeful behaviour across substrates.

- **Status:** abstract-only. Deprioritized behind the empirical papers because it's a theory proposal without (from the abstract) an implementation yet — the abstract itself says the co-emergence idea "has not yet been computationally formulated." **Flag:** interesting for the goals/agency thread and worth a full read if we go deeper on goal-directed agent design; note the overlap with the empowerment paper (both make goals primary).

- **Abstract:** Purposeful behaviour is a hallmark of natural and artificial intelligence, often thought to rely on world models with descriptive (what is) and prescriptive (what is desirable) aspects. Canonical accounts like RL posit distinct components — a state representation and a reward function. An alternative, not yet computationally formulated, is that these co-emerge interdependently from an agent's goal. The authors describe a framework of goal-directed state representation in which both aspects co-emerge from experiences, introducing 'telic' states (classes of goal-equivalent experience distributions) drawing on Buddhist epistemology, giving a parsimonious account of goal-directed learning as statistical divergence between policies and desirable experience features.
