- **One-line take:** Block's surgical distinction: "**consciousness**" names two different things that get fatally conflated. **P-consciousness** (phenomenal) = experience itself, the "what it's like" (Nagel's target). **A-consciousness** (access) = a state's content being *poised* for use in reasoning, verbal report, and rational control of action — a purely functional notion. The confusion: taking evidence about *access* to be evidence about *experience*.

- **Why the distinction is the useful tool:** A-consciousness is exactly what functional/computational systems can have and what behavioural tests measure (report, integration, control). P-consciousness is what Nagel/Chalmers say those tests can't reach. Naming both stops the equivocation where "we explained consciousness" slides between "we explained information access" (true, tractable) and "we explained experience" (the hard problem, untouched).

- **Block's caution:** many "functions of consciousness" arguments in psychology/neuroscience quietly establish a function of **A-consciousness** and then claim to have illuminated **P-consciousness**. That inference is the titular "confusion." He argues the two can in principle come apart (conceptually, and maybe in real cases like some neurological syndromes).

- **Why it's foundational to this issue:** it's the precision instrument for the whole "is there an 'I' in AI?" cluster. Every RSTA claim about machine minds is really a claim about **A-consciousness** (information poised for control/report — which agents demonstrably have degrees of); the leap to P-consciousness is what Nagel/Chalmers block. Sits between them and Dennett (who'd resist a sharp P/A joint) as the analytic mediator.

- **Why it matters for us:** the single most practical vocabulary item for talking about SmolPaws honestly. An agent that routes internal state into reasoning, reporting, and action control is a good candidate for **access**-consciousness-like organization — a real, buildable, testable property. That says **nothing** about phenomenal experience. Using Block's two words keeps every capability claim ("the agent is aware of its context / its own state") precise and non-inflationary.
