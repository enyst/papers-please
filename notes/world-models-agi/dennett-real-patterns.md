- **One-line take:** Dennett's answer to "are beliefs/minds *real* or just useful fictions?" — a third option. **A pattern is real iff there's a description of the data more efficient than the raw bit-map** (compressibility as the criterion of reality). Patterns can be *real* without being *fundamental*. This is the deflationary counterweight to Nagel/Chalmers, and the philosophical backbone for taking "emergence" seriously without mysticism.

- **The core definition (worth keeping verbatim):** "A pattern exists in some data — is real — if there is a description of the data that is more efficient than the bit map, whether or not anyone can concoct it." Reality is not all-or-nothing and not observer-dependent in a deflating way: the compression either exists or it doesn't. But which *real* pattern you track depends on your interests (you often want lossy compression that preserves "the pattern that matters").

- **The intentional stance payoff:** treating a system as having beliefs/desires (the intentional stance) earns its keep when it yields massive predictive compression over the physical/design stances. So mental states are **real patterns** — not spooky substances (contra dualism), not mere fictions (contra eliminativism), but genuine regularities visible from the right stance. A middle path between "minds are fundamental" and "minds are illusions."

- **Where it sits in the debate:** Dennett is the issue's implicit **anti-hard-problem** pole. Against Chalmers/Nagel he'd say: once you've explained all the real patterns (the functions, the reportability, the self-model), there is no further "hard" residue — the intuition that there is, is an artifact. You don't have to agree, but the shelf is one-sided without it.

- **Why it's foundational to this issue:** it's the rigorous underpinning of the RSTA **emergence** paper ("More is Different" = higher-level real patterns describable by lower-dimensional effective variables — that *is* Dennett's compressibility criterion) and of the **levels-of-analysis** paper (each Marr level tracks real patterns at a different grain). "World model" itself, on this view, is a real pattern an agent exploits for prediction.

- **Why it matters for us:** a working criterion for when an agent's abstraction is *real* vs confabulated — does it compress the data / improve prediction? That's a usable test for concept formation and memory: a good learned concept is a real pattern (it buys compression + predictive leverage), not just a stored string. It also licenses talking about SmolPaws' "self" as a real pattern (a predictively useful stance) without overclaiming phenomenal experience — the honest middle Dennett is built for.

- **Famous line:** patterns are real when they afford "a description of the data that is more efficient than the bit map."
