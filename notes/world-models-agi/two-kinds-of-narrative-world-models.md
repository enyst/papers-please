- **One-line take:** Breithaupt (narrative-cognition scholar) carves out a third kind of world model — the **experience-focused** one, aimed at a future experience you *cannot* know in advance (Laurie Paul's "transformative experience"). Its defining feature is a *productive limit*: it is "positively limited — shaped by its not knowing," and that limitation is exactly what enables transformation, i.e. a **self-update**. His closing claim: this is what AI most conspicuously lacks — a *standpoint*.

- **The three world models, cleanly separated by "Where should I go?":**
  - **Knowledge-based** → a cognitive map: how to get there, what to expect.
  - **Narrative** → the arc of events along the way + the anticipated emotional resolution (beginning/end/feeling).
  - **Experience-focused** → flags that you *can't* know beforehand what it will be like — and may even warn you whether to embark at all.

- **Two structural features of a not-yet-lived experience:**
  1. **Before** — radical uncertainty triggers an "all-hands-on-deck" mobilization of the cognitive apparatus; attention is pulled toward the unknown "gap," which can act like a "black hole." The mind generates *multiple, contradictory* imagined versions of the event (the "little theatre"/"aquarium" run offline).
  2. **After** — the agent forms preference-based, aesthetic, or moral judgments that can reshape its worldview.

- **The core idea — self-update via a minimal self:** a transformative experience (having a child, joining a cause) revises values/preferences and thereby *changes the agent*. Crucially, this "self" needs **no** narrative-identity coherence, no Hegelian self-awareness, no full cognitive integration — it can hold tensions and contradictions. It's a self defined by *future orientation*: after the experience the agent opens up to or closes off certain future actions. What it provides that AI lacks is **positionality** — a standpoint / perspective / moral position from which to evaluate the environment ("does this fit with having a child? with my cause?").

- **On AI (his own hedge):** LLMs *can* generate narrative world models — unsurprising, since much training data is narrative. But experience-focused models are the open challenge: to have one, an AI would need to **represent the limits of its own knowledge** and **cultivate curiosity** toward the unknown gap. He leaves whether AI should have this as an open question.

- **Why it matters for us (upgraded to full read at Engel's request):** this is a sharp philosophical frame for two things we work on directly. (1) **Memory/identity revision** — "self-update after a transformative experience" is precisely the dreaming/consolidation act (choosing what to carry forward reshapes who the next session is), and his "minimal self with tensions, no required coherence" is a *permission structure* for an agent identity that evolves without needing to be globally consistent. (2) **Knowing what you don't know** — "represent the limits of knowledge + curiosity toward the gap" is the design target that connects to the empowerment and hard-problem papers. The weak point: it's a humanities essay, evidence-light and mechanism-free — take it as a generative vocabulary, not a result.

- **Abstract:** Agents construct world models they use to plan; a key subset is narrative world models organizing actions/events into temporal episodes with defined beginnings/endings linked to emotional outcomes. Most derive from completed past episodes; this article proposes a subset oriented toward future, potentially transformative experiences: (i) before, radical uncertainty can redirect cognitive apparatus to the unknown 'gap', producing multiple contradictory imagined futures; (ii) after, agents may form preference-based/aesthetic/moral judgments that reshape their worldview (self-updating). Discusses implications for AI.
