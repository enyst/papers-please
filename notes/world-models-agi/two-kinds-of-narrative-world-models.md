- **One-line take:** Narrative world models organize events into episodes with beginnings, endings, and emotional outcomes. The paper's novelty is a *second* kind aimed at future, transformative experiences: before, radical uncertainty triggers an "all-hands-on-deck" imaginative search over contradictory futures; after, aesthetic/moral judgments can reshape the worldview (self-updating).

- **Status:** abstract-only — phenomenology-leaning, more conceptual than mechanistic, so deprioritized behind the empirical/agent-relevant papers. **Flag:** the "transformative experience → self-update" idea is a nice frame for agent memory/identity revision (cf. our dreaming/consolidation work), if we want the philosophical angle.

- **Abstract:** Agents construct world models they use to plan; a key subset is narrative world models organizing actions/events into temporal episodes with defined beginnings/endings linked to emotional outcomes. Most derive from completed past episodes; this article proposes a subset oriented toward future, potentially transformative experiences: (i) before, radical uncertainty can redirect cognitive apparatus to the unknown 'gap', producing multiple contradictory imagined futures; (ii) after, agents may form preference-based/aesthetic/moral judgments that reshape their worldview (self-updating). Discusses implications for AI.
